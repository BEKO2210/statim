Statim plays: one forward pass per move

Each turn a small game engine describes every legal move in one plain sentence. Statim reads them with a one-line strategy and returns a probability for every move; the highest is played. A 13 MB LoRA adapter taught it from the decisions of a hand-written engine heuristic. No LLM, no search. These are the median games of 50 evaluation seeds the adapter never saw.

    How it works

    Three steps per move. The engine supplies facts, Statim weighs them.

    01

    Engine lists moves with features

    A small game engine enumerates the legal moves, simulates each one step ahead and writes the result as a sentence: merges, empty cells, distance to the food, discs flipped, holes.

    Move left merges two 64 tiles
    into a 128; 6 cells stay empty.
    02

    Statim scores every move in one pass

    The sentences go to Statim as one choice question with the strategy as its instruction, through the actions LoRA adapter. One request, one forward pass, a probability for every move.

    POST /v1/systemone · adapter: actions
    median 19 ms per move on an RTX 3070
    03

    Highest probability is played

    The move with the highest probability is applied, the engine advances the game, and the next decision starts.

    argmax p → "left"
    engine applies the move

    What it shows

    A small adapter teaches the decision model a new kind of choice

    • Without the adapter Statim is close to chance in Snake, Othello and Tetris: it follows the wording of the move names, not the sentences.
    • With it, Snake and Othello reach the teacher’s level: in Snake it eats 2.5 more food per game than its teacher on average (95% interval +0.2 to +4.9), which is not significant per game (sign test p = 0.11).
    • 2048 and Tetris stay below the teacher; a second training run with more 2048 and Tetris games is in progress.
    • One forward pass per move, median 19 ms on an RTX 3070 (p95 31 ms).
    What it does not show

    No search, no pixels, no self-play

    • The engine does the arithmetic: it simulates every move one step and writes the result as a sentence. Statim weighs the sentences; it never sees pixels.
    • No lookahead: no tree search, one decision at a time.
    • Imitation, not reinforcement learning: the adapter learned from the heuristic’s choices, so the heuristic bounds it.
    • Replays: the page plays recorded games; the median-score game of the 50 seeds is shown for each game, not the best one.

    * 12 of the heuristic’s 50 Tetris games were stopped at 5,000 moves, so its mean is a lower bound. Snake food, Othello disc margin and 2048 score are the engines’ own scores. Protocol, per-seed results and code: demos/actions.
