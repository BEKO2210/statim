# Statim on S1Bench (2026-10-03)

Protocol, fixed before the run: [s1bench-protocol.md](s1bench-protocol.md). Raw records (every
item's prediction, truth and top probability) and the overlap check are in
[`bench/results/s1bench/`](../../bench/results/s1bench/); the tables below are the output of
`bench/s1bench_compare.py` on them.

## Short version
- **Statim Decide Multilingual 0.7.0 scores 0.638 macro over all 13 subsets.** Lev (Qwen3.5-4B + LoRA,
  GPU) scores 0.689 and Jev 0.761 under the same harness. **Statim is behind both.**
- Against the untuned Laya checkpoint it was fine-tuned from, Statim gains 5.9 points macro, all of it
  on intent routing (MASSIVE), safety (aegis2) and summary rating (SummEval), and **loses on reading
  comprehension and minimal-pair tasks**: PAWS significantly (0.764 → 0.656, Holm p = 0.014), and
  boolq, squad2, vitaminc and MultiNLI in the same direction without reaching significance. The
  fine-tuning traded reading comprehension for decision categories; the next model has to win it back.
- On the six subsets of the third-party board snapshot Statim scores 0.648, between Laya (0.625) and
  decider-2b (0.703): rank 7 of 21 entries, ahead of Qwen3-8B (0.535) and every GLiNER and RWKV entry.
- Calibration: mean ECE 0.138 (Laya 0.179, Lev 0.115, Jev 0.091).
- Our Laya run scores 0.630 on the six board subsets where the board lists Laya at 0.625, so this
  harness reproduces the board's measurement of the same checkpoint.

| Harness | `levbench` (lev @ `745535b`), backend `lev`, concurrency 1, timeout 120 s; tasks from `lev s1bench export` (3,880 items); `bench/s1bench_run.py` keeps every record |
| Lev, Jev | levbench numbers quoted from Lev's `docs/FINDINGS.md`, not re-measured. The "published" Jev values that circulate with S1Bench equal Nimble's own measurement of Jev; we found no TypeSafe publication of them. |

## In-domain subsets and overlap
Statim 0.7.0 was trained on the train splits of MASSIVE, MultiNLI and Aegis 2.0 (S1Bench uses their
test or dev splits), so massive-en-US, massive-de-DE, multinli and aegis2 are in-domain for Statim
and not for Lev, which excluded all 13 sources. The exact-overlap check (state strings of at least 30
characters against the 590,346 strings of the training mixture) finds 15 aegis2 items and 1
helpsteer2 item; the MASSIVE training split had already dropped every text that also occurs in its
test split. Removing the 16 items changes no macro by more than 0.002.

## Reproduce
```sh
git clone https://github.com/Abhinavexists/lev && cd lev && git checkout 745535b && uv sync
uv run --with "datasets>=3.2" --with "huggingface-hub>=1.0" lev s1bench export --out data/s1bench
statim serve -m multilingual=statim-decide-multilingual-base-f32.gguf --port 8080 &
uv run python <statim>/bench/s1bench_run.py --tasks data/s1bench --base-url http://127.0.0.1:8080 --out s1.json
python3 <statim>/bench/s1bench_overlap.py --tasks data/s1bench --mixture data/mixture-v8.jsonl.gz --out overlap.json
python3 <statim>/bench/s1bench_compare.py s1.json <laya-run>.json --overlap overlap.json
```
